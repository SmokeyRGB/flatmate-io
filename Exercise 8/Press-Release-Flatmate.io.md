### Flatmate.io Launches to End the WhatsApp Chaos of Choosing a New Roommate

Flatmate.io launched today to solve a problem every shared flat in Germany knows by heart: picking a new flatmate turns into weeks of scattered chats, missed messages, and one exhausted person doing all the work while everyone else quietly falls behind. The new platform brings the whole process into one place, so it's no longer a burden for one person and a blur for everyone else.

Anyone who's lived in a WG with five or more people has seen both sides of this. A room opens up, and within two or three weeks dozens of applications flood in through a listing portal, WhatsApp, email, and word of mouth. Someone, usually whoever posted the ad, ends up copying each one into the group chat, tallying reactions, wrangling seven calendars into a viewing slot, and eventually writing back to everyone involved. It's a job nobody signed up for, and it doesn't get lighter the third time it happens that year.

"I'd get home from work and there'd be twelve new messages about a vote I hadn't even seen open," said Lukas Berger, a resident of a seven-person WG in Tuebingen and an early tester of the platform. "It wasn't that I didn't want to participate. I just never knew where things stood, so eventually I stopped checking."

That's the other half of the problem Flatmate.io was built to solve. The organizer is buried in coordination. Everyone else is just trying to keep up with their own week, and a decision that unfolds entirely inside a group chat punishes anyone who steps away for even a few days. Flatmate.io answers both at once: one pass entering an application turns it into a permanent card instead of a chat message, so there's no more manually tallying reactions or digging up the same application every time someone asks about it again. A live vote count and a clear "what's needed from you" view mean a resident coming back after a busy week can catch up in under a minute instead of scrolling through hundreds of messages. Results stay hidden until each person votes, so returning late doesn't mean voting after already seeing everyone else's opinion, and a private veto in the second round makes sure someone who missed the viewing but has a real concern still gets heard.

"Running our last few rounds nearly burned me out, and I could see everyone else checking out at the same time," said Samuel Zink, founder of Flatmate.io. "It really is about making the organizer's job easier. But just as much, it's about the other six people, who shouldn't have to choose between their own lives and having a say in who they live with."

The clearest sign it's working will be participation itself: the share of eligible residents who actually vote per round, targeting above 80 percent, up from the two or three who typically respond over WhatsApp today. Getting there means nobody has to be taught how to use it first: the organizer shouldn't need to walk six flatmates through a new app, and a resident should be able to just vote, no explanation required. Flatmate.io is free for residents permanently, funded through donations, and runs as an installable web app.

The team is now onboarding its first households in Tuebingen. For now, the goal is close to home: nobody organizes this alone, and nobody gets left out just because their week got busy.
